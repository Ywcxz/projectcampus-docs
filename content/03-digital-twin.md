# Digital Twin

*A living model of the campus, and the university's long-term aspiration*

A digital twin is an interactive model of the campus: its buildings, rooms, roads, and utilities, the offices in them and what they handle, and what is happening across the grounds. It has its own chapter beside the network because it could become a project as large as the network itself. [Established] (D-081) The author sees it as the long-term aim that would serve students best, possibly as a full-scale model built with VSU's own spatial data, and expects it is what VSU would want most from a digital campus; whether VSU shares that aim, and which data and tools it would need, are open. [Unresolved] (Q-75) It keeps the conditions that held it back as a later direction: it needs spatial data, operational integration, and infrastructure far beyond any first product. [Established] (D-077)

VSU's Main Campus spreads 188 buildings over 61.6 hectares of grounds, among them 21 student dormitories and 17 academic buildings. [Established] ([Chapter 4](04-intranet.md)) In 2015, VSU's orientation for new students included a campus walk, led by USSO staff, the student councils, and peer facilitators. [Established] (E-060) The walk is over in an afternoon, but the questions keep coming for weeks: where to replace a lost ID, where to request a transcript, which office signs which form. [Proposed] The twin's first, smallest step would keep that walk going: a student starts from her question and gets the office, its room and hours, what to bring, and the way there, whenever she needs it. [Proposed] (D-079) The grounds are also VSU's research: root and tuber crops such as cassava, sweet potato, and taro are studied at PhilRootcrops, set up at VSU in 1977, and abaca at the National Abaca Research Center. [Established] (PhilRootcrops, n.d.; NARC, n.d.) Inside the VSU Nature Park lies the first demonstration site of rainforestation farming, a way of restoring forests with native trees, developed at VSU under a project that ran from 1989 to 1999 and adopted by the Department of Environment and Natural Resources as a national strategy in 2004. [Established] (ELTI, 2018; Asio, 2017) A student walking past sees fields and trees; the twin could also tell her what is studied there, by which program, and where to ask. [Proposed] (D-192)

> **In short.** The twin's first step is not a model but the network's first places: each place keeps one record of what it is, where it is, and the way there, and the network adds who can help there. [Proposed] (D-191) On grounds that are also VSU's research, a place could show what is studied there, with its owners' consent and never its people, and it can be tried first as a labeled mock-up that needs nothing from VSU. [Proposed] (D-192) A three-dimensional model comes last, built from VSU's own spatial data once an office would use it, because a virtual campus built before what fills it can end up empty. [Proposed]

## A path, not a project

Each step stands on its own, and none commits VSU to the next. [Proposed]

```text svg=twin-path
The twin's path, step by step
1  Places and the way there      ─┐  the first step, built as
2  Where to go for what          ─┘  the network's first places
   └─ What is studied here           research only; a labeled mock-up first
3  VSU's spatial data                if VSU shares it
4  Status offices already keep       linked where VSU permits
5  Sensors                           deferred
   Then three-dimensional models, simulation, and live dashboards,
   once an office would use them
```

[Proposed] (D-079; D-191; D-192; Q-49)

1. **Places and the way there.** Buildings, rooms, offices, laboratories, dormitories, the VSU Market, gates, and landmarks, with the names people actually use, and walking directions between them with landmarks rather than only a line on a map. Stored on the phone, so it works with weak signal ([Chapter 4](04-intranet.md)). [Proposed] These are the network's first places, not a separate guide: a building or landmark that has a place on the network has its location here, and the twin and the network keep one list of places rather than two ([Chapter 2.2](02b-network-design.md)). [Proposed] (D-144; D-191)
2. **Where to go for what.** A person starts from a question, such as replacing a lost ID or requesting a transcript, and gets the office, its room and the way there, its hours, what to bring, and the official system to use if there is one, each citing the Citizen's Charter or the office with the date it was last confirmed. [Proposed] The network's "reach the right office" use case would point here ([Chapter 2.1](02a-use-cases.md)). [Proposed]
3. **VSU's spatial data.** The GIS inventory's layers, if VSU shares them. [Unresolved] (Q-49)
4. **Status offices already keep.** Room bookings, outages, and maintenance, linked where VSU permits. [Proposed]
5. **Sensors.** Energy, water, and occupancy readings carried on the campus network's later stage ([Chapter 4](04-intranet.md)). [Deferred] VSU named campus-wide environmental monitoring and connected lab equipment among the aims of its IPv6 work in 2024. [Established] (E-044)

Places are the shared spine of CAMPUS: each physical place would have one record, in which the twin holds what it is, where it is, and the way there, the network adds who can help there and what is posted, and the receptionist is the door into both. [Proposed] (D-191; D-144; D-181) Built this way, the first step would be made once, by the network's first place, rather than twice. [Proposed] (D-178; D-191) What is studied here, below, would grow from the same places and would need none of step 3's data. [Proposed] (D-192)

The network's later modules build on the early steps: Housing shows distance and the way to campus from the twin's places, and the marketplace's meeting points are places in it ([Chapter 2](02-social-network.md)). [Proposed]

## What is studied here

By VSU's own account, CHED identified it in 2000 as a Center of Excellence in Agriculture, Agricultural Engineering, and Forestry. [Established] (E-061) Which of those designations is still current has not been confirmed. [Unresolved] Some of the work in these fields is done in the open, as at the rainforestation site in the Nature Park, so a place on the twin could show what is studied there. [Proposed] (D-192)

Picture a first-year student at the edge of a crop plot. She opens the twin on her phone and sees what is planted, how far along the trial is, which program runs it, and a link to the place on the network where she can ask about it. [Proposed] (D-192) In the Nature Park, a tree shows its species and that it stands on the first rainforestation site. [Proposed] She never sees who planted either. [Proposed] (D-192) Nor does she see who is there now. [Established] (D-089; D-093)

Each entry would hold five things. [Proposed] (D-192)

- **What is studied**, in a line, such as a sweet potato variety trial or a native-tree restoration plot.
- **Who runs it:** the program or unit, never a person's name, and its stage: planned, running, or finished.
- **Where to read more:** a link to published work, rather than a summary of it.
- **Where to ask:** a link to the place on the network where people can be asked ([Chapter 2.2](02b-network-design.md)).
- **When it was confirmed:** the date, and the unit that confirmed it.

An entry would appear only with the consent of those who own the work, students included, and unpublished research would show only what its owners and VSU allow. [Proposed] (D-192) Some sites are safer left vague, such as rare trees or the places where wildlife is studied. [Proposed]

Agriculture and Forestry lead, by the author's choice: two of VSU's three designations are in those fields, and research centers such as PhilRootcrops and the National Abaca Research Center work in agriculture. [Proposed] (D-192; E-061) Engineering follows: Agricultural and Biosystems Engineering, whose field holds the third designation, with work such as an irrigation trial; Geodetic Engineering, whose survey control points would also anchor the twin's own coordinates; and Mechanical Engineering, with work such as a test rig. [Proposed] (D-192) Mechanical Engineering is the author's own program, and it is included because he can supply his own work as the first real examples. [Proposed] (D-192)

It would serve a first-year who walks past the fields without knowing what they hold, a student choosing a major, a project, or a thesis site, and visitors, and it would give faculties and research centers a way to show their work where it happens. [Proposed] Whether students want this, and whether faculties want their work shown, has not been asked. [Unresolved] An entry would link to a research center's own page rather than restate it as its own authority. [Proposed]

Other universities already map part of this. On Michigan State University's campus tree map, instructors and researchers can work with the university's botanical garden to record which trees they use for courses and research, so that others can filter the map to those trees. [Established] (Beal Botanical Garden, n.d.) What is studied here would go beyond trees, to crop plots, forest stands, survey marks, and test rigs across several fields, and would point to the network's places, where people can be asked. [Proposed] (D-192)

It is research only, beyond the twin's first step. [Established] (D-081) Until it is taken up, it would be shown only as a mock-up: real public places, with made-up entries labeled as made up. [Proposed] (D-192; D-102; D-108) The places could come from OpenStreetMap. [Proposed] Its data may be copied and adapted with credit to OpenStreetMap and its contributors, data altered or built upon may be shared only under the same licence, and it asks contributors never to copy from copyrighted maps, such as Google Maps, without permission. [Established] (OpenStreetMap Foundation, n.d.) A mock-up like this needs no permission from VSU; real entries need their owners' consent and VSU's agreement. [Proposed] (D-192)

Its standing cost is people: each entry would need an owner who confirms it every semester, or it would be marked out of date. [Proposed] Entries would live with the network's places, on open components VSU could host. [Proposed] (D-191; [Chapter 2.2](02b-network-design.md)) It would be disproved if faculties decline to show their work, if students open it only in their first weeks, or if entries go stale faster than their owners confirm them. [Proposed]

## What waits

Three-dimensional models, simulation, and live sensor dashboards wait until the earlier steps show an office would use them. [Deferred] In the light review, campus twins serve estates and energy teams first, and the student-facing use is closer to a guide to places, which is why that guide comes first here. [Proposed]

Building the world before what fills it has gone wrong before: James Madison University built a campus in the virtual world Second Life in 2008, and by its authors' account it failed in April 2012, after four years of successful use. [Established] (Stevens and Stevens, 2012) A 2026 review of digital twins in engineering education found the evidence more promising for understanding than for behaviour, where it is still tentative, and found long-term effects not yet studied. [Established] (Coombs, Soban, and McCartan, 2026) Another review names high costs, infrastructure, and faculty training as the barriers. [Established] (Kotecha et al., 2025) Three-dimensional views can help when they serve a task: the University of Manchester's 3D scans of its music and drama centre gave students a virtual induction before they used its rehearsal rooms. [Established] ([Chapter 10](10-related-work.md)) So the model would follow its content: places, the office guide, and what is studied here come first, and a three-dimensional view earns its place only by showing them better than a list does. [Proposed]

### What a model would be built from

A twin has three layers. Geometry is the shape of the grounds and buildings, which laser scans and drones can capture. Meaning is what each place is, who is responsible for it, and what happens there, which only people can give. Life is what changes: schedules, notices, and readings. [Proposed] The network's places, tied to real units and looked after by stewards, are the meaning layer, which is why the first step starts there and not with geometry. [Proposed] (D-144; D-191)

For geometry, VSU already holds a good deal: its Phil-LiDAR projects produced flood hazard maps and resource maps for about 40 municipalities and cities in Eastern Visayas. [Established] (Rappler, n.d.) In 2017 it was among the partner universities that signed an agreement to share LiDAR data. [Established] (DREAM, 15 July 2017) Its GIS laboratory was formally launched in December 2000, and in January 2025 it created a Geospatial and GIS Research and Services Center. [Established] (VSU GIS Services Unit, n.d.; [Chapter 2](02-social-network.md)) Whether VSU would share any of this for a model, and on what terms, is open. [Unresolved] (Q-49; Q-75) Students can build such geometry themselves: at Wake Forest University, classes used one drone, laser scans, and teams on foot to map more than 1,000 campus trees across about 90 acres. [Established] (Wake Forest University, 31 August 2026) At VSU, geodetic engineering and forestry projects could do the same for the twin, with VSU's permission for the flights and the data. [Proposed]

An open format would keep the choice of software open. [Proposed] 3D Tiles, adopted as a community standard by the Open Geospatial Consortium in 2019, can be drawn by CesiumJS or by other 3D engines, such as three.js. [Established] (Cesium, 5 February 2019) Which format and which renderer a model would use is open. [Unresolved] (Q-75)

Detail would grow in steps, each used before the next is built. [Proposed]

1. Places with their meaning, in two dimensions, on phones.
2. Simple blocks built from building footprints and heights.
3. A drone survey of the core campus.
4. Interiors, only where an office or a study needs them.
5. Live data, which waits with the sensors in step 5 of the path above.

The author's design notes for a later model are these. [Proposed]

- An isometric view, which shows a building's height and floors, and keeps nearby boarding houses from crowding into one label at a wide zoom.
- The campus and the barangays around it, not a map of the world.
- Trees and plots placed by surveyed coordinates, such as RTK GNSS, rather than by hand.

When the author tried measuring the campus by hand in Google Earth, it proved slower than expected, which is one reason the model waits for VSU's own spatial data. [Unresolved] His own 3D sketches so far use Roblox Studio, chosen because it runs on the computers available to him; they are sketches, not a choice of engine for VSU's model. [Unresolved] (Q-75)

### The spatial interface

The author's longer-term idea is a way into the network itself: browsing CAMPUS by place as well as by list, so that a building shows what is posted in its own place and in the places located there, such as notices, questions, and events. [Proposed] (P-05, P-13) The familiar list would remain for anyone who prefers it. [Proposed]

It stays research only, behind the first step. [Established] (D-093) It needs step 1's places and the network's places with their locations before it has anything to show, it would cost far more to build and keep current than a list, and nothing yet shows that finding things by place beats a list or search for campus tasks. [Proposed] It maps places and what is posted about them, never people. [Established] (D-093)

It would be disproved if people in a test find what they need faster, and with fewer mistakes, in a list than on the map, or if they use the map only in their first weeks on campus. [Proposed]

## Open questions

- Would VSU share its GIS inventory and the Citizen's Charter in a usable form, and who would keep office information current? (Q-49)
- Would the faculties of Agriculture and Forestry, and VSU's research centers, want their work shown on the twin, and who in each would confirm its entries? [Unresolved]
- Would the Geospatial and GIS Research and Services Center and the Department of Geodetic Engineering share base data for a model, and in which open format? (Q-75)
- Does VSU itself aim for a digital twin, and which office would own one? [Unresolved]
- What will OneVSU cover for students? (Q-18)
- Does VSU, or do its students, already run or plan a campus map or directions service that the first step should build on instead? (Q-54)
- Would browsing the network by place help people find things faster than a list or search? [Unresolved]
- Which places should not be mapped in detail, among them research sites such as rare trees or the places where wildlife is studied? [Unresolved]
